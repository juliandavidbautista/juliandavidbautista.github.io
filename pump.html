Fluid mechanics / Prototype testing

Submerged impeller pump

We built a centrifugal pump powered by two AA batteries and tested how much water it could lift. A small alignment error in a bicycle-spoke shaft helped explain the gap between the design curve and the bench results.

Keeping the motor dry

Our brief called for 100 mL in under a minute at a head of at least 20 cm. We compared a shaft seal, a magnetic coupling and a submerged impeller with the motor above the reservoir. The last option scored 4.15 out of 5 in our weighted decision matrix. Submerging the inlet also eliminated the need to prime the pump.

We printed the 30 mm, six-vane impeller, volute and cover with its guide column in PETG. The printed coupling slipped, so we joined the motor shaft to the spoke with epoxy putty. That solved the grip problem but made concentricity difficult to control.

What the bench showed

We timed a 100 mL collection at each outlet height. The prototype reached shutoff at 14 cm, below the 20 cm target. Interpolating between the 9 and 12 cm tests gives about 43 seconds at 10 cm.

At 9 cm, about 2.5 mW reached the water. Using the single submerged electrical reading of 2.9 V and 1.40 A, we estimated a peak measured efficiency of 0.062%. Current rose from 0.22 A in air to 1.40 A under load. We traced the most likely loss to a spoke set off-centre in the putty, with leakage at the volute cover contributing. Our next revision would use a concentric sleeve coupling and a sealed cover.
